# Suno — provider research, August 2026

**GEN-005. Research only. No code was written and no adapter exists.**

The §48 order places Suno research after generation hardening. This is that
research, and its conclusion is that the ticket after it cannot be started yet.

---

## How good this evidence is

**Read this before relying on anything below.** This session's network egress
allows web *search* but blocks direct fetches of `suno.com`, `help.suno.com`,
`musicbusinessworldwide.com` and `en.wikipedia.org`. So:

- **No primary source was read directly.** Not Suno's terms, not its help
  centre, not the trade-press articles themselves.
- What follows rests on **search-engine summaries of those pages**, which is a
  weaker thing than the pages.
- Every commercial claim below — rights by plan tier, download caps, indemnity —
  **must be re-verified against `suno.com`'s actual terms** before a single
  track generated through Suno is distributed. This document is a starting
  point for that check, not a substitute for it.

Recording the limitation rather than presenting the findings as settled fact is
the point. A rights claim sourced from a search summary is exactly the kind of
thing that looks authoritative in a document and is wrong at the distributor.

## 1. Is there an official API? No.

As of August 2026 Suno has **no public, self-serve API**: no developer console,
no documentation site, no OpenAPI specification, no published request or
response shapes, no stated rate limits, no pricing.

There has been movement. In **July 2026** Suno's Chief Product Officer said the
company is *exploring* a developer API, starting with a curated group of
partners recruited through an intake form, and said it is particularly
interested in applications that "unlock experiences generative music makes
possible for the first time."

That is an exploration, not a launch. There is **no announced timeline**, no
self-serve access, and no documentation.

### What this means for the ticket after this one

It cannot start. §5's rule — *use only official API contracts; do not guess
model names, accepted formats, file limits, response fields, timestamp
capabilities or rate limits* — is not a preference that can be relaxed when a
vendor is slow. With no published contract there is nothing to write an adapter
**against**.

### The unofficial wrappers are not an option, and this is not a close call

Every commercial result for "Suno API" is a third-party service that
reverse-engineers Suno's web client and resells access. The standing constraint
is explicit: **never integrate unofficial reverse-engineered Suno wrappers.**

That constraint is also correct on its own merits, which is worth saying so it
is not obeyed as a rule nobody understands:

- Such a wrapper works by holding **a Suno account's session credentials** and
  driving the private web client. Handing an operator's account to a third party
  is the whole mechanism, not a side effect.
- It breaks whenever Suno changes its client, which is not a stable foundation
  for a catalogue that must still be distributable next year.
- It is very likely a terms-of-service violation for the account being driven,
  and the account is the one that holds the **commercial rights** to the output
  (see §2). A route that risks the account risks the rights to everything made
  through it.
- SaniTube's distribution path asserts commercial rights to a distributor.
  Sourcing audio through a channel the supplier has not sanctioned makes that
  assertion one nobody can stand behind.

**No exception is recommended and none should be granted on schedule pressure.**

## 2. Commercial rights — the part that actually matters here

SaniTube already models this: `CommercialRightsStatus` on every generation, and
`StartMusicGeneration` sets it to `UNKNOWN` unconditionally, because a
generation that has not happened cannot have been cleared for sale. This
research says what the eventual answer would have to be derived from.

Subject to the evidence caveat above:

| Question | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Who owns output? | The user, if they were **subscribed to a paid plan at the moment the song was created**. |
| Does that survive cancellation? | Reported yes — rights attach at creation and persist. |
| Free tier? | **Non-commercial only.** No distribution, no monetisation. |
| Retroactive? | **No.** Subscribing later does **not** grant rights over songs made while free. |
| Copyright registration? | Fully AI-generated music likely **cannot** be registered under current US law. The protection is *contractual*, from Suno's terms — not copyright. |
| Indemnity? | **None reported.** If a third party claims infringement, the user carries the risk and the defence costs. |
| Download caps | Pro subscribers reported limited to a monthly download allowance, tightened during 2026 under the Warner agreement. |

### Three consequences for the platform, if an adapter is ever built

1. **Rights are a property of the moment of generation, not of the account
   today.** A per-generation record is required, and the existing
   `ProviderTermsSnapshot` model is the right shape for it. Reading "is this
   account subscribed?" at distribution time would answer a different question
   from the one that matters.
2. **`UNKNOWN` must stay the default and must never be inferred.** Nothing about
   the *existence* of a generation establishes its terms. The current code is
   right and should not be relaxed to "assume cleared if a key is configured".
3. **A download cap is a hard operational bound on a production plan.** GEN-004
   ceilings count *requests*. If a provider separately caps how many results may
   be **retrieved** per month, a plan can succeed at generating and still fail at
   collecting. That is a distinct limit and is not covered by anything built so
   far.

## 3. Legal context, stated because it bears on distribution

SaniTube's purpose is to deliver recordings to distributors. Whether the audio's
provenance is contested is therefore a platform question, not merely the
supplier's problem.

- **November 2025** — Warner Music Group settled its copyright suit against Suno
  and signed a licensing deal, reported as the first of its kind. Under it,
  training material must be licensed, and users pay to download tracks.
- **Universal Music Group's suit against Suno is ongoing.** **Sony has settled
  with neither** Suno nor Udio.
- Suno has asked a federal court to block UMG and Sony from adding **more than
  61,000 recordings** to their infringement claim.
- **June 2026** — the American Federation of Musicians sued UMG and Warner,
  alleging member recordings were licensed to Suno and Udio without payment or
  credit; later amended. Warner has moved to dismiss.
- **June 2026** — Suno raised a further $400m at a reported $5.4bn post-money
  valuation, while still facing those suits.

This is not a reason to refuse to build an adapter, and this document does not
recommend that. It is a reason that **`CommercialRightsStatus` must remain a
recorded fact per generation rather than a platform-wide assumption**, and that
the terms snapshot must capture what was true on the day — because what is true
is, demonstrably, still moving.

---

## 4. What would unblock the next ticket

An adapter becomes writable when **all** of these hold:

1. Suno publishes a **public API contract** — endpoints, authentication, request
   and response shapes, error codes, limits — that this platform can read
   without an NDA.
2. Access is obtainable **without reverse engineering**, whether self-serve or
   through the partner programme.
3. The **terms for output generated via the API** are stated, so
   `ProviderTermsSnapshot` records something real rather than a guess.
4. Any **retrieval or download cap** is stated, so it can be modelled alongside
   GEN-004's request ceiling rather than discovered in production.

Until then the correct state of the platform is the one it is already in:
`generation.default` is `none`, the fake provider drives every path end to end,
and `GEN-002` remains an external blocker.

**Nothing in this document changes any code.** The two things it does change are
that the blocker is now *specific* — an unpublished contract and unverified
output terms, rather than "no credentials" — and that the download-cap risk in
§2.3 is written down before it can surprise a production plan.
